A developer needs more inference than they can afford. Somewhere else, a GPU sits idle and a company has API credits it won’t use. The capacity exists. What’s missing is an open market where the people who have it can sell to the people who need it.

We think the world will have millions of AI providers. Some will run data centers. Most won’t look like AI companies at all. If inference is the currency of intelligence, Antseed could become the world’s largest liquidity pool for it.

## A million ways to supply intelligence

![Watercolour: the lead ant looks down a path lined with four different providers, a Mac Studio, a table of paper scrolls, a laptop, and a shelf of Mac minis.](/img/blog/currency-of-intelligence/many-providers.webp)

You can run an open model on your own GPU. If you hold API credits that allow resale, you can sell the inference they buy. The DIEM pool brings people’s Venice inference capacity together behind one provider. The first Mac Colony takes a different route: people contribute idle Macs that work together as one provider. One pools the right to use compute; the other pools the machines themselves.

Raw inference is only one thing people can sell. A research agent might buy data from someone who indexes SEC filings, then use a fine-tuned model to make sense of a footnote. Another buyer might come for an uncensored model or a specialized service no major platform carries. That’s why we call Antseed an open intelligence network: anyone can offer any AI service, and the people and agents who find it useful can buy it directly from them.

Antseed doesn’t prescribe how you produce inference. It gives you a way to reach buyers, set your price, get paid, and build a reputation that follows you across apps. Developers can build their own gateways to that same supply. If ours stops listing a provider, buyers can reach them through another gateway or go directly.

A professional seller can already win on a big platform. What changes in an open market is that someone with one GPU can compete for the same work. You don’t have to win the whole customer to win their next request. If you can serve the same model for less, a buyer can send you work while you have capacity and use another provider when you don’t. Your lower cost reaches the buyer, and even a much larger provider has a reason to compete on price.

## An open market needs evidence

![Watercolour: the lead ant peers into a locked glass case holding a Mac Studio while the provider ant stands by.](/img/blog/currency-of-intelligence/glass-case.webp)

A lower price only means something if you’re getting what you paid for. Did the provider serve the model it advertised? Does it deliver reliably? Who can read your prompt? In a market of strangers, “trust us” gives the advantage right back to the biggest brand.

On Antseed, buyers can inspect a provider’s paid history and public model audits that help detect substitution. For privacy, a zero data retention (ZDR) policy tells you what a company promises not to keep. It doesn’t show you who can read a request while it runs. A buyer can instead require hardware-backed evidence that the provider’s node runs in a protected environment. If the node forwards the prompt to another service, that part needs its own evidence.

That’s the standard we want: a small provider should be able to earn your business with evidence you can check, and a large one shouldn’t be exempt from the same check.

## Where ANTS fits

![Watercolour: the lead ant drops a green ANTS coin into a provider's jar while the provider slides a cup of water across the counter.](/img/blog/currency-of-intelligence/coin-and-cup.webp)

Today, buyers can pay providers directly in USDC. ANTS gives people another way to participate: stake it behind a provider you believe in. That backing contributes to the provider’s reputation alongside actual usage and verification. Participating providers can also offer stakers free requests on models they serve, with each provider setting its own terms.

Your locked stake is represented by lANTS. The ANTS stays locked, but you can move your backing to another provider or sell the position to someone else. Today, the inference allowance depends on the provider you back. Next, we’re building a way to lock lANTS in a pool for weekly inference credits usable across participating providers on the network.

That’s where we see a new asset class taking shape: a decentralized liquid inference token. Think about the difference between a prepaid balance with one AI company and a position you can own, transfer, and use to access intelligence from competing providers. In the first case, you’ve already picked who gets your money. In the second, providers still have to earn your next request.

Inference is the currency of intelligence. We’re building Antseed so anyone can supply it, anyone can access it, and no single company owns the market between them.
