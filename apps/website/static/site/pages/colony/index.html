How it works

Many machines.
One Colony.

A Colony brings people’s computers together to serve AI on Antseed. To everyone using Antseed, it is one provider.

Your Mac joins. Antseed Colony is a menu bar app. Your Mac joins a Colony and downloads the model it runs.

The Colony works as one provider. Each request goes to a Mac in the Colony that can answer it, and faster, steadier Macs get more of the work.

Payments settle onchain. Every payment a buyer signs names what it has paid your Mac, and the Colony’s contract splits each settlement between its Macs.

You claim. The app shows what your Mac has earned. Claim it to your own wallet when you like.

Frequently asked
questions.

What is a Colony?

A Colony is a group of machines working together to serve AI on Antseed. An operator coordinates the Colony, and each machine is paid for the answers it gives.

Can I join the Mac Colony today?

Early access is invite-only while we test. Sign up with your Mac’s details and we’ll reach out with an invite to the early group.

Sign up →
Can I download the app now?

Not yet. Early members get it from our team in the early group.

Which Macs can join?

Macs with Apple silicon on macOS 14 or later. The first Colony serves a large model, so it starts with Mac Studios with 256 GB or more.

Can I still use my Mac?

Yes. Pause sharing from the menu bar any time.

Does my Mac need to stay on?

Your Mac serves while it’s awake and online. The app can open at login and keep the Mac awake while it shares.

How do I get paid?

Your Mac is paid for the answers it gives. The app shows what it has earned, and you claim it to your own wallet.

Does it cost anything to join?

No. Joining is free.

Can I build my own Colony?

Not yet. Building a Colony of your own is coming soon.

Can the pool operator read prompts or keep my earnings?

The operator routes only ciphertext, and the pool contract pays workers from what the buyer signed for them. The operator still decides which Mac gets work.

Is this a TEE?

No. The Mac keeps its keys in the Secure Enclave, but memory is not encrypted and the Mac’s owner can still read prompts in the inference engine. For hardware-encrypted inference, use TEE sellers.
