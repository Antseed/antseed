08 Characteristics of the crypto-asset

ANTS is the native token of the AntSeed protocol, implemented as an ERC-20 token on the Base blockchain. It is designed to be used to access features of the protocol. These features are functions of the protocol, not rights attached to the token itself, and each requires separate action by the holder; simply holding ANTS does not confer any entitlement.

Payments for AI services on the network are made in USDC, a US dollar stablecoin, and not in ANTS. ANTS is a reputational and separate incentive mechanism: the protocol creates new ANTS on a scheduled basis and allocates it to participants based on their verified activity on the network, such as completed service payments. These allocations require active participation and are not received through passive holding.

Offering paid services on the network does not require a stake; providers are paid in USDC for services rendered regardless of whether any ANTS is staked. A stake is relevant only to establish eligibility for ANTS rewards. That eligibility stake is denominated in ANTS and held in seller pools attached to a provider's on-chain identity, and any ANTS holder may stake in support of a provider. A legacy mechanism under which eligibility was established through a USDC-denominated stake remains available as a fallback until it is explicitly disabled.

The token has a maximum supply of 1.04 billion ANTS. As at 29 September 2026, approximately 120 million ANTS had been released under the emission schedule, of which approximately 118.67 million (98.9%) had been minted, including 3 million ANTS allocated to the burn address. Transfers of ANTS are currently restricted; transferability is targeted for Q4 2026, although no date has been fixed. New tokens are released on a fixed weekly schedule of 5 million ANTS per week, with the weekly amount halving every 104 weeks. Each week's release is allocated across fixed and dynamic components: 15% to the Foundation and 15% to contributors are fixed regardless of network activity, while up to 10% to verification and other growth incentives, up to 40% to stakers and up to 10% each to buyers and providers scale with network activity. Up to 30% of that week's scheduled release that is not earned through actual network activity shall be permanently removed from circulation ("burned"). The remaining balance shall be allocated to the Foundation. A portion of the Foundation’s allocation has been committed to the institutional and venture-capital investors through private sales, subject to lock-up and vesting restrictions.

The protocol charges a 4% fee on AI service payments. These fees accumulate in a separate reserve contract. Accumulated fees may in future be directed to purchasing ANTS on the open market and permanently removing the purchased tokens from circulation (a "buy-and-burn" mechanism). This is not currently operating, and its activation and terms depend on the protocol's contracts and governance parameters.

The AntSeed protocol operates on a decentralised, non-custodial and open-source basis, without a central operator or centralised user onboarding; participants interact directly with the protocol's smart contracts. Funds are held in smart contracts under rules fixed in code and not by the Foundation or any other person.

D.4 Crypto-asset project description

Purpose and Goals:

AntSeed is an open-source, peer-to-peer network that allows users to buy AI services directly from independent providers, without a central platform in the middle. Providers can offer AI models, APIs or specialised agents, while buyers access these services through their existing AI tools. The project's goals are to enable open, competitive pricing for AI services, to allow participants to keep control of their own funds at all times, and to coordinate participation in the network through ANTS, the native token of the AntSeed protocol.

Key Features and Operation:

  • Direct connections between buyers and providers. Buyers find providers over the torrent network and exchange requests and responses with them directly over encrypted connections, without a central server or account system.

  • Works with existing AI tools. AntSeed's software (Virtual Private Router (VPR) and the AntSeed command-line tool) lets buyers connect the AI applications they already use to the network. The software automatically finds and selects providers on the buyer's behalf, based on preferences the buyer sets, such as price, speed, reliability and reputation.

  • Direct on-chain payments. Buyers pay providers directly in USDC (a US dollar stablecoin) through smart contracts on the Base blockchain. Funds are held in smart contracts under rules fixed in code, not by the Foundation or any other person: the channel contract holds no USDC, and funds remain in the deposit contract until charged or withdrawn. The protocol charges a 4% fee on these payments, which accumulates in a separate reserve contract. Once ANTS becomes transferable, these fees, including the amounts accumulated to date, will be used for the ANTS buy-and-burn mechanism.

  • The ANTS token. ANTS is the native token of the AntSeed protocol, designed to be used to access features of the protocol. It is separate from the payment system described above: buyers pay providers in USDC, while ANTS is created by the protocol and allocated to active participants based on their verified activity. Offering paid services on the network does not itself require a stake; sellers are paid in USDC for services rendered regardless. Eligibility for ANTS rewards, however, requires a seller pool with sufficient staked weight. This eligibility stake has historically been denominated in USDC under a legacy staking mechanism, and an upgrade under which it is instead denominated in ANTS, held in seller pools, has been developed and independently audited. Under this upgrade, the legacy USDC stake remains a fallback path to eligibility of providing ai services until it is explicitly disabled. Scheduled allocations not earned through actual network activity are permanently removed from circulation ("burned"), up to 30% of that week's scheduled release, with any remainder allocated to the Foundation.

  • Identity and reputation. Each provider has an identity on the network linked to its operator. A provider's reputation is built from its stake, its record of completed services, and feedback from buyers, and is used by buyers' software when selecting providers.

  • Independent verification. Approved verifiers test providers by sending them sample requests and publish the results on-chain in a form that anyone can independently check, helping buyers assess the quality and honesty of providers.

Past milestones

AntSeed has launched a decentralised, open marketplace for AI services in which independent providers offer hundreds of AI models through self-hosted software, with buyers paying providers directly in USDC on the Base blockchain and no intermediary holding user funds. The network is live and operational: as at 29 September 2026 it had processed approximately 189 billion AI tokens and settled approximately USD 302,860 in payments for AI services, with 208 providers and 1,573 buyers active on the network.

The distribution of ANTS is also already operational. ANTS, the native token of the AntSeed protocol, is designed to be used to access features of the protocol. Newly created ANTS is released on a fixed weekly schedule and allocated to active participants, the Foundation and contributors. Offering paid services on the network does not itself require a stake; providers are paid in USDC for services rendered regardless. A stake is required to be eligible for ANTS rewards, and ANTS can already be staked into seller pools for this purpose; this stake has historically been denominated in USDC under a legacy mechanism, which remains a fallback path to eligibility for providing ai services until it is explicitly disabled. During this period, ANTS is not transferable: participants can claim and stake ANTS but cannot trade it.

Future milestones

  • Q4 2026 (target): ANTS becomes transferable. ANTS is expected to become freely transferable during Q4 2026, enabling holders to trade it and to use it across the protocol's features without restriction.

  • Following transferability: activation of the buy-and-burn mechanism. H.1 Distributed ledger technology (DTL)

H.3 Technology used

AntSeed uses a non-custodial model in which users control their own wallets and funds, with settlement occurring directly on-chain, currently in USDC on Base, via smart contracts. The node that participates in the network has a single secp256k1 key used as its signing identity and PeerId, while the funding wallet holding assets can be any external wallet the user controls, including hardware wallets, externally owned accounts, or multisig wallets. On the desktop app, the node's private key is stored encrypted using the operating system keychain, via Electron safeStorage. CLI and server setups store a plaintext key file by default but can instead load the key from an environment variable backed by an external secrets manager. Payments are authorised via EIP-712 signatures from the signing identity and settled directly to provider wallets, with no central custodian holding user funds.

H.4 Consensus mechanism

AntSeed runs on the Base blockchain, where ANTS is an ERC-20 token on Base mainnet and payments settle in USDC on Base. Base itself is an Ethereum Layer 2 chain using an optimistic rollup architecture built on the OP Stack, batching many transactions off-chain and posting them to Ethereum for final settlement.

Security comes from Base inheriting Ethereum's Proof of Stake security guarantees: rollup transaction data is posted to Ethereum, which provides data availability and finality, while the optimistic model allows fraud proofs to challenge invalid state transitions. Efficiency comes from aggregating many user transactions into batches before posting to Ethereum, meaning users benefit from significantly lower fees and higher throughput than transacting directly on Ethereum L1, while still ultimately relying on Ethereum for settlement.

H.9 Audit outcome

The AntSeed protocol's smart contract technology has undergone an independent security audit conducted by Sherlock, carried out between 3 July and 18 July 2026, with the final report issued on 12 August 2026. The audit covered the protocol's "Recognized Usage" upgrade to its trust and reputation layer, reviewing twelve Solidity smart contracts across the protocol's core, emissions, payments, and seller modules, including the ANTS token contract, the emissions gate, seller pools and staking, usage accounting, and payment channel contracts.

The audit identified a total of nineteen findings, categorized by severity as two High, four Medium, and thirteen Low or Informational. As of the final report, no findings remained both unfixed and unacknowledged. Both High-severity findings and all four Medium-severity findings were resolved. Of the thirteen Low/Informational findings, four were resolved and nine were formally acknowledged by the development team, with the acknowledged items comprising minor or deliberate design decisions that the team assessed as acceptable or scheduled for a future protocol upgrade.

The High-severity findings related to edge cases in the payment channel logic that could have allowed a buyer to receive paid services while avoiding collectible payment; both were remediated through contract and seller-node fixes. The Medium-severity findings concerned agent-to-seller binding, reward-weight timing, emissions minter management, and retroactive configuration effects on past epoch rewards, all of which were resolved.

The auditor notes that the audit does not constitute a guarantee or warranty regarding the security of the protocol. The audit covered the contracts implementing this upgrade. The staking contract currently in use on the network was not within the audit scope.

I.5 Technology-related risks

Smart contracts AntSeed relies on multiple smart contracts deployed on Base mainnet, including the ERC-20 ANTS token, an AntseedDeposits contract that custodies buyer USDC and seller payouts, an AntseedChannels contract that manages per-session payment locking and settlement (and which holds no USDC itself), and the recognized usage protocol which includes the smart contracts governing ANTS distribution. This concentrates smart-contract risk in a relatively complex settlement flow (reserve-serve-settle), where bugs or logic errors in deposit custody, channel accounting, or emission logic could lead to loss or misallocation of funds. Funds are held by smart contracts under rules fixed in code rather than by the Foundation or any other person; the channel contract holds no USDC, and funds remain in the deposit contract until charged or withdrawn. Because the deposit contract holds pooled user funds, a contract-level exploit there represents a worst-case exposure affecting all deposited funds across all users.

Cross-chain and protocol interactions The current design is single-chain for on-chain settlement, using USDC on Base mainnet (chain ID 8453), with a Base Sepolia testnet deployment for testing; there is no documented cross-chain bridge, so classic bridge risk is limited, but any future expansion to other chains or wrapped assets would introduce additional attack surfaces. The protocol also depends on external AI providers and their infrastructure, so misconfiguration or compromise at the provider level can cause incorrect or malicious responses even if on-chain components behave correctly.

Scalability and performance Routing and metering run over an encrypted peer-to-peer network with explicit, hard-coded limits on frame size, concurrent connections per IP, upload/stream caps, and stream durations, which mitigates some resource-abuse and denial-of-service vectors but may become bottlenecks under heavy load or targeted attack. Settlement depends on Base's throughput and gas costs; spikes in network congestion could delay settlement, increase operating costs for providers, or affect the economics of small-value transactions.

Wallets, keys, and identity Each node's identity is a single secp256k1 private key whose derived EVM address serves simultaneously as the network PeerId and the on-chain signing address; there is no key-derivation or multi-key scheme, so this one key signs protocol messages, EIP-712 payment authorizations, and on-chain transactions. The architecture separates this signing identity from an external funding wallet: the funding wallet deposits USDC into the AntseedDeposits contract and has no ongoing role thereafter, which bounds worst-case loss from a compromised node to the current deposit balance rather than the full funding wallet. This nonetheless places significant weight on secure storage of the signing key. In desktop deployments the key is encrypted at rest via the operating system keychain, but in CLI/server mode the default is a plaintext key file, making operational security practices (environment-variable injection, secrets managers, hardened hosts) critical.

Privacy The network is designed for anonymous, account-free usage, with confidentiality of requests and responses resting substantially on transport-level encryption between peers. Buyers who select providers without hardware-based attestation rely primarily on transport encryption and provider honesty for prompt and content confidentiality. Where the protocol offers attested (TEE-based) providers, part of the privacy assurance shifts to correct hardware attestation and configuration.

Layer 2 (Base) dependencies All token and payment flows use Base and USDC, so AntSeed inherits Base's consensus, uptime, and censorship-resistance properties; outages, reorganizations, or protocol changes on Base could halt deposits, settlement, or ANTS transfers. Regulatory or issuer actions affecting USDC on Base (such as freezes or blacklisting by the stablecoin issuer) could also impair users' ability to fund the network or withdraw funds.

Audits and security assurance The AntSeed smart contracts underwent an independent security audit conducted by Sherlock in July 2026 (final report August 2026), covering the ANTS token, payment channel, deposit, staking, and emissions contracts. This included the on-chain validation of EIP-191/EIP-712 signatures and associated nonce and timestamp replay protection, as these checks are implemented within the audited contracts. All High and Medium severity findings were resolved, and remaining Low/Informational findings were either resolved or formally acknowledged by the development team. The act of signing itself, meaning the client-side generation of these signatures and the security of the underlying signing key, falls outside the scope of a contract audit and is addressed instead under key-handling practices described above. Separately, penetration testing of the peer-to-peer node software has not been completed per project documentation, meaning the node software, including its resource-usage limits and other network-level controls, has not undergone equivalent independent review.

I.6 Mitigation measures

Smart contracts
The protocol's smart contracts on Base mainnet, including the ANTS token, deposit, payment channel, staking, and emissions contracts, were reviewed in an independent security audit conducted by Sherlock in July 2026. All High and Medium severity findings were resolved prior to the final report, and remaining Low/Informational findings were either resolved or formally acknowledged by the development team. The audit specifically strengthened the payment channel settlement logic, closing edge cases in the reserve-serve-settle flow through both contract-level and node-level fixes. The architecture also isolates custody risk: the AntseedChannels contract that manages session logic holds no USDC and is designed to be swappable (redeployable by re-pointing the on-chain registry), while the fund-holding contracts (Deposits, Staking) are treated as stable contracts that rarely change. All deployed contracts are verified on BaseScan.

Cross-chain and protocol interactions
Confining on-chain settlement to a single chain (Base) avoids cross-chain bridge dependencies and the associated attack surface, as no bridge or wrapped-asset mechanism is used in the current design. Contract addresses for each supported chain are built into the client software rather than manually configured, reducing the risk of misdirected transactions.

Scalability and performance
The peer-to-peer transport layer enforces hard-coded resource limits, including a maximum frame size (64 MiB), per-request upload cap (32 MiB), global pending upload cap (256 MiB), stream buffer and duration limits, request and upload timeouts, and a per-IP inbound connection cap, which constrain resource exhaustion and denial-of-service conditions. Transport applies fail-closed handling on decode errors. Settlement is designed around Base's low-cost Layer 2 environment to limit transaction-cost exposure.

Wallets, keys, and identity
The protocol structurally separates the node's signing identity from fund custody: the signing key never holds funds directly, and an external funding wallet (which may be a hardware wallet or multisig) deposits into the AntseedDeposits contract and has no ongoing role thereafter. This bounds the worst-case loss from a compromised node to the current deposit balance, leaving the funding wallet unaffected. In desktop deployments, the signing key is encrypted at rest through the operating system keychain, with any legacy plaintext key auto-encrypted on upgrade. For server and CLI deployments, the documentation directs operators to supply the key via an environment variable backed by a secrets manager rather than storing it on disk, and recommends funding only via the deposit contract from a hardware wallet or multisig, depositing only amounts needed per session.

Cryptographic and protocol controls
Trust-critical operations are cryptographically verified: node identities use secp256k1 keypairs, with protocol messages, connection handshakes, and metering receipts signed using EIP-191 personal_sign and verified via ecrecover, and payment authorizations signed using EIP-712 (ReserveAuth and SpendingAuth), each scoped to a specific seller, capped by a maximum amount, and time-bounded by an expiry deadline. Connection envelopes include nonce and timestamp checks to resist replay attacks, and payment is gated (via HTTP 402) until an on-chain reservation is committed. Emission rewards are subject to per-recipient caps, with overages redirected to the Protocol Reserve.

Layer 2 (Base) dependency
The dependency on Base and USDC is inherent to the design and is disclosed as a residual risk rather than one that can be fully mitigated; the single-chain approach nonetheless avoids the additional attack surface introduced by multi-chain or bridged architectures.

Residual and outstanding items
Penetration testing of the peer-to-peer node software has not yet been completed per project documentation, and is identified as an outstanding assurance item. Certain acknowledged (unremediated) low-severity audit findings reflect deliberate design decisions or fixes deferred to a future protocol upgrade, as recorded in the audit report.

S.4 Consensus mechanism

AntSeed runs on the Base blockchain, where ANTS is an ERC-20 token on Base mainnet and payments settle in USDC on Base. Base itself is an Ethereum Layer 2 chain using an optimistic rollup architecture built on the OP Stack, batching many transactions off-chain and posting them to Ethereum for final settlement.

Security comes from Base inheriting Ethereum's Proof of Stake security guarantees: rollup transaction data is posted to Ethereum, which provides data availability and finality, while the optimistic model allows fraud proofs to challenge invalid state transitions. Efficiency comes from aggregating many user transactions into batches before posting to Ethereum, meaning users benefit from significantly lower fees and higher throughput than transacting directly on Ethereum L1, while still ultimately relying on Ethereum for settlement.

S.5 Incentive mechanisms and applicable fees

Incentive mechanisms (ANTS emissions)

ANTS releases are tied to authenticated network activity rather than to proof-of-work mining. The protocol releases 5 million ANTS per week, in periods referred to in the protocol as "epochs," with the weekly amount halving every 104 weeks.
Each week's release is allocated across fixed and dynamic components. Two allocations are fixed regardless of network activity: 15% to the Foundation and 15% to contributors. The remaining allocations are dynamic and scale within defined ranges according to network activity: up to 10% to verification and other growth incentives, rewarding participants who validate network activity such as behavioural model checks, TEE attestation and anti-abuse work, 2% to 40% to seller-pool stakers, based on the amount of ANTS actively staked in seller pools relative to a network-wide stake target, and 5% to 10% each to providers and to buyers, based on recognised settled USDC volume relative to a network-wide usage target.
Each allocation is administered by a distribution contract holding a share of that week's release. Any portion of a dynamic share that is not earned through verified network activity is processed through a remainder mechanism: ANTS is minted to a burn address first, up to 30% of that week's scheduled release, with any remaining amount minted to the Foundation's reserve address.

Network security model

Offering services and being selected for paid traffic does not itself require any stake; providers are paid in USDC for services rendered regardless. A stake is relevant only to establish eligibility for ANTS rewards. This eligibility stake is already denominated in ANTS, held in seller pools bound to a provider's on-chain agent identifier, and any ANTS holder, including buyers, may stake ANTS in support of a provider. A legacy mechanism under which the eligibility for providing ai services stake was denominated in USDC remains available as a fallback path until it is explicitly disabled; under that legacy mechanism the minimum stake was 10 USDC.
ANTS itself is currently non-transferable, so there is no liquid market in ANTS; releases accrue to eligible participants and the Foundation according to the schedule above. Network security and reward eligibility are governed through the stake requirement combined with on-chain reputation and feedback signals, rather than through block production.

Applicable fees

A 4% protocol fee, denominated in USDC, is charged on settlement of AI service transactions between buyers and providers and accumulates in a separate reserve contract; it is not paid to a centralised company. As at 29 September 2026, approximately USD 12,030 had been collected. The 4% protocol fee will be used for the ANTS buy-and-burn once ANTS becomes transferable. The burning of unearned weekly releases described above is a separate mechanism and does not fund any purchase of ANTS.
